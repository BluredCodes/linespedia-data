Adjacent from the train station in Largs
We came upon a timid dirt trail
And having blissfully bulged our bellies
With brackish crisps and Irn-Bru,
We took to this beguiling artery. 

Even before the ochreous soft drink
Had supernaturally resurfaced in ghostly aftertaste, 
Thick hands of jade had descended
To shield our ears from the wheezy speech 
Of roaring cars and civilization.

With eager tongues we thankfully licked 
The effervescent seal of this evergreen envelope.

Each breath we breathed was boarded
By moss and moisture and forest floor,
Crisp air never before held prisoner
By any living human lung.
From countless waterfalls it rose
To wipe our cheeks revived and ruddy
Like a mother with a moistened napkin.

Further we meandered into the dense woodland, 
Each tree a kindly old sage
Festooned with luscious lime beards of lichen,
Willing that we wander to the wisdom
That they have freely offered for ages
To the unhearing ears of man.

With difficulty we crossed a rustling pool,
Laboriously leaping like leprous mainlanders
From narrow rock to narrow rock
Nipped more than once by the earth’s frosty sap.
There stood a solemn wall of living stone.

On its face a thousand lines,
Spoke clearer than any dialect,
Of what was and what shall be
Eyewitness of the birth of the world,
Eyewitness of impending passing. 

Our hike had come to sudden finish,
For the big stone man had spoken
Reminding us that nothing lasts forever
